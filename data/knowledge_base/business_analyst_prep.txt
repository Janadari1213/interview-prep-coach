=== BUSINESS ANALYST INTERVIEW KNOWLEDGE BASE (20+ DEMO QUESTIONS & MODEL ANSWERS) ===

Q1: What is the difference between Functional and Non-Functional Requirements?
Model Answer: Functional Requirements define WHAT the system should do (e.g., "The system shall process credit card payments"). Non-Functional Requirements specify HOW the system should perform (e.g., SLA latency under 200ms, 99.99% availability, ISO security compliance).
Evaluation Criteria: Clear distinction between business features vs quality attributes (usability, reliability, performance).
Coaching Tips: Provide concrete examples of each type and explain why non-functional requirements dictate technical architecture.

Q2: How do you bridge the gap between technical developers and non-technical business stakeholders?
Model Answer: I act as a translator using clear domain models, user stories, Wireframes, and Business Process Modeling Notation (BPMN). I facilitate alignment workshops, establish shared vocabulary, and validate acceptance criteria to ensure business intent matches technical execution.
Evaluation Criteria: Stakeholder management, communication adaptation, requirements traceability, and active listening.
Coaching Tips: Emphasize using visual diagrams (use case diagrams, flowcharts) rather than technical jargon.

Q3: What is the difference between Business Requirement Document (BRD) and System Requirement Specification (SRS)?
Model Answer: A BRD focuses on business goals, problem statements, and high-level ROI objectives created from the business perspective. An SRS translates BRD goals into detailed technical specs, data flow diagrams, API interactions, and system behavior for developers.
Evaluation Criteria: Target audience differentiation, level of abstraction, and requirements lifecycle mapping.
Coaching Tips: Highlight that BRD answers "WHY" and "WHAT", while SRS details "HOW" systems behave.

Q4: How do you perform Gap Analysis in a Business Process?
Model Answer: Gap Analysis evaluates the difference between current state ('As-Is') and desired future state ('To-Be'). Steps include: 1. Mapping As-Is workflows. 2. Defining target To-Be capabilities. 3. Identifying missing features, bottlenecks, and data gaps. 4. Formulating a prioritized remediation roadmap.
Evaluation Criteria: As-Is vs To-Be process mapping, root cause identification, and actionable roadmap creation.
Coaching Tips: Walk through a practical example like transitioning from manual paper approvals to automated digital workflows.

Q5: Explain the INVEST criteria for writing effective User Stories.
Model Answer: INVEST stands for Independent (can be built separately), Negotiable (details discussed in refinement), Valuable (delivers clear user benefit), Estimable (can be sized by developers), Small (fits within a sprint), and Testable (has explicit acceptance criteria).
Evaluation Criteria: Agile methodology mastery, story sizing, and acceptance criteria formatting (Given-When-Then).
Coaching Tips: Write a sample user story: "As a customer, I want to filter products by price, so that I can find affordable items."

Q6: How do you conduct Requirement Elicitation with stubborn or conflicting stakeholders?
Model Answer: I conduct 1-on-1 interviews, facilitated alignment workshops, and MoSCoW prioritization sessions (Must, Should, Could, Would). I ground decisions in data, business impact metrics, and user research to resolve conflicting opinions objectively.
Evaluation Criteria: Conflict resolution, facilitation techniques, objective metrics usage, and consensus building.
Coaching Tips: Discuss active listening techniques and focusing stakeholders on core business outcomes.

Q7: What is BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) and why is it useful?
Model Answer: BPMN is a standardized graphical notation for modeling business workflows. It uses swimlanes, tasks, events (start/end), decision gateways (exclusive/parallel), and message flows to create clear, standardized visual documentation accessible to both business and technical teams.
Evaluation Criteria: BPMN symbol knowledge (gateways, events, pools/lanes) and process optimization experience.
Coaching Tips: Explain how decision gateways prevent ambiguous workflow paths.

Q8: How do you define Acceptance Criteria using the Given-When-Then (BDD) format?
Model Answer: Given-When-Then defines explicit conditions of satisfaction: 'Given' [initial state/precondition], 'When' [user action/trigger], 'Then' [expected system output/outcome]. Example: Given a user on checkout, When valid payment is submitted, Then display order confirmation.
Evaluation Criteria: Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) criteria, clarity, and developer testability.
Coaching Tips: Provide an edge-case example (e.g., expired credit card handling).

Q9: What is Root Cause Analysis and how do you use the 5 Whys technique?
Model Answer: Root Cause Analysis identifies underlying causes of business operational problems rather than patching surface symptoms. The 5 Whys iteratively asks "Why did this happen?" five times until the foundational process or systemic flaw is revealed.
Evaluation Criteria: Problem-solving depth, 5 Whys methodology, and Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram usage.
Coaching Tips: Walk through a real supply chain delay or bug outbreak using 5 Whys.

Q10: How do you handle Scope Creep during project execution?
Model Answer: I enforce formal Change Control procedures. When new requirements are requested: 1. Analyze impact on budget, schedule, and resources. 2. Present impact to the Change Control Board (CCB) / Product Owner. 3. Swap lower-priority backlog items or adjust delivery dates before accepting scope changes.
Evaluation Criteria: Change management process, impact analysis, backlog grooming, and stakeholder management.
Coaching Tips: Reframe "saying no" into "offering trade-off choices with transparent impact analysis".

Q11: What is a Use Case and what are its core components?
Model Answer: A Use Case describes a interaction sequence between an Actor (user/system) and the application to achieve a specific goal. Key components include: Actor, Preconditions, Trigger, Main Success Scenario, Alternate/Extension Flows, and Postconditions.
Evaluation Criteria: Actor definition, happy path vs alternate paths, and postcondition verification.
Coaching Tips: Outline a Use Case for 'User Resets Forgotten Password'.

Q12: How do you write SQL queries for Data Analysis (JOINs, GROUP BY, Aggregations)?
Model Answer: I use SQL for data profiling and business metrics validation. Key operations include INNER/LEFT JOINs to combine tables, `WHERE` for filtering, `GROUP BY` for data aggregation (SUM, COUNT, AVG), and `HAVING` for filtering aggregated groups.
Evaluation Criteria: SQL syntax proficiency, relational logic, and data analysis capability.
Coaching Tips: Write a sample query finding top 5 customer segments by total revenue.

Q13: What is the difference between Agile (Scrum/Kanban) and Waterfall Methodologies?
Model Answer: Waterfall is a sequential, phase-gated approach (Requirements -> Design -> Code -> Test) best for fixed-scope projects. Agile is an iterative, incremental approach delivering working software in short sprints, allowing continuous feedback and changing requirements.
Evaluation Criteria: Agile vs Waterfall trade-offs, sprint cadence, and backlog prioritization.
Coaching Tips: Explain when Waterfall is appropriate (regulatory/hardware) vs Agile (software/SaaS).

Q14: How do you create Wireframes and User Process Flows?
Model Answer: I map User Process Flows to establish screen transitions, then construct low-fidelity Wireframes using tools like Figma, Balsamiq, or Miro. These visual mockups validate UI layout, user journeys, and data fields with stakeholders early before coding.
Evaluation Criteria: Wireframing tools, user experience flow mapping, and early stakeholder validation.
Coaching Tips: Explain the benefits of low-fidelity wireframes in preventing costly late code changes.

Q15: What is MoSCoW Prioritization Technique?
Model Answer: MoSCoW categorizes requirements into: Must Have (non-negotiable MVP core), Should Have (important but not critical for initial release), Could Have (desirable enhancements if time permits), and Won't Have (agreed out of scope for current release).
Evaluation Criteria: Prioritization framework mastery, MVP scope management, and stakeholder alignment.
Coaching Tips: Emphasize that Must Haves should not exceed 60% of total team capacity.

Q16: How do you measure KPI and ROI success for a newly implemented business solution?
Model Answer: I establish baseline metrics prior to launch, then track Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) post-launch (e.g., user adoption rate, task completion time reduction, error rate reduction). ROI is calculated as `(Net Benefits - Implementation Cost) / Implementation Cost * 100`.
Evaluation Criteria: Metric quantification, baseline comparison, and business outcome measurement.
Coaching Tips: Give a concrete example: reducing order processing time from 15 minutes to 2 minutes.

Q17: What is SWAT / SWOT Analysis in Business Strategy?
Model Answer: SWOT Analysis evaluates internal Strengths and Weaknesses along with external Opportunities and Threats. It provides a strategic framework to leverage organizational advantages, mitigate risks, and capitalize on market opportunities.
Evaluation Criteria: Strategic analysis, internal vs external evaluation, and risk mitigation planning.
Coaching Tips: Demonstrate how SWOT informs product strategy decisions.

Q18: How do you facilitate User Acceptance Testing (UAT)?
Model Answer: 1. Develop UAT test plans and scripts based on business acceptance criteria. 2. Train business end-users. 3. Manage UAT execution sessions and defect logging. 4. Validate bug fixes with developers. 5. Obtain formal business sign-off before production deployment.
Evaluation Criteria: UAT test script creation, defect triage, business user training, and sign-off governance.
Coaching Tips: Emphasize that UAT validates business fitness, not just technical bug-free code.

Q19: Explain Risk Analysis and Risk Assessment Matrix.
Model Answer: Risk Analysis identifies potential project risks and categorizes them by Likelihood (High, Medium, Low) and Impact (High, Medium, Low). High-impact/High-likelihood risks are assigned explicit mitigation plans, contingency budgets, and risk owners.
Evaluation Criteria: Risk identification, probability-impact matrix, and mitigation strategy formulation.
Coaching Tips: Provide mitigation examples: fallback vendors, feature toggles, or automated backups.

Q20: How do you handle incomplete or ambiguous requirements from stakeholders?
Model Answer: I conduct discovery sessions, analyze existing system data, build prototype wireframes to stimulate feedback, and ask clarifying probing questions. I document assumptions clearly and obtain explicit stakeholder validation before finalizing specifications.
Evaluation Criteria: Proactive clarification, assumption documentation, and iterative requirements refinement.
Coaching Tips: Detail your step-by-step approach to turning vague ideas into concrete specs.
